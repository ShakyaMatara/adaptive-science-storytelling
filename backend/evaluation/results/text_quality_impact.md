## FINDING-3 - doubled characters from overprinted bold text

The textbooks render bold type by drawing the same glyphs twice with a small offset. `pdfplumber` recovers both impressions, so every character of an affected run appears twice. The defect is invisible in the PDF and only surfaces once the text layer is extracted.

## Severity - how much of an affected chunk is actually corrupted

The incidence figures above count a chunk as affected if it contains ANY corrupted
token, which overstates the harm: most affected chunks are ordinary English prose
carrying a short glossary tail or a single bold run. Banding by the share of
corrupted words gives a fairer picture. Characters the books use legitimately
(superscripts, degree, micro, maths operators, arrows, the bullet glyph) are
excluded, so the bullet character that opens many list items is not counted as
corruption.

| Corrupted share of chunk | Chunks in index | % | Retrieved passages (k=8) | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean (0%) | 837 | 76.0% | 254 | 79.4% |
| trace (<2%) | 82 | 7.4% | 11 | 3.4% |
| light (2-10%) | 120 | 10.9% | 33 | 10.3% |
| heavy (>=10%) | 62 | 5.6% | 22 | 6.9% |

Corrupted chunks are retrieved at approximately their base rate in the index, so
the defects are neither preferentially retrieved nor preferentially avoided.
The share of retrieved passages that is *heavily* corrupted - the band where the
text would be unusable as grounding - is **6.9%**, and
**76.0%** of the index is entirely clean.
